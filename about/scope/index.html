ABOUT / SCOPE

A Mathematical
Sky Survey.

A systematic search through the open questions between mathematics’ landmarks.

SCOPE — the System for Conjecture and Open-Problem Exploration — is a research map rather than a publication stream. It continuously indexes structured, confidence-labelled records across the mathematical landscape: unresolved cases, useful constructions, sharper bounds, and connections waiting to be made.

Most survey results are intended to remain quiet catalogue entries, becoming visible primarily when they are relevant to a researcher exploring the corresponding region of mathematics. Only results that pass stronger originality, correctness, and significance checks are promoted beyond the catalogue.

THE OBSERVING CYCLE

From a question to a record.

The planned workflow separates finding a result from establishing what it proves.

01
SELECT

Find a tractable open question.

Identify a precisely stated question and map its assumptions, sources, and known boundaries.

02
SCREEN

Check what is already known.

Search prior work before committing research effort. Record the scope and limitations of that search.

03
EXPLORE

Give SCOPE a bounded observing window.

SCOPE Survey coordinates research under explicit time and cost limits, producing a claim and a traceable argument.

04
REVIEW

Examine the result from three directions.

Separate reviews assess correctness, originality, and mathematical value. A promising claim alone does not count as a confirmed result.

05
FORMALIZE

Bring valuable results to the kernel.

Selected high-value results enter Lean verification. The record states the exact formalized claim, its assumptions, and external dependencies.

06
CATALOGUE

Keep the map open.

Place the core derivation, provenance, and verification status in the searchable catalogue. Supporting code and research material remain available in the annual GitHub archive; only high-value records are promoted beyond the map.

REVIEW & VERIFICATION

See what stands behind a result.

Independent AI review is required before a SCOPE research result is published, and is recorded in its detail-page sidebar. Catalogue entries and search filters highlight two additional checks: Lean Verified and Expert Attested. These are complementary evidence categories, not interchangeable levels of certainty.

AI Reviewed
An AI reviewer independent of the producing research agent has reviewed the full stated result and its proof. The review record identifies the reviewer, date, scope and proof version. This is not a mathematical guarantee.
Lean Verified
A formalization covering the full stated result has passed Lean kernel checking. The evidence identifies the checked declaration and proof version, with the statement correspondence, assumptions and axiom dependencies available for inspection. Checking a supporting lemma alone does not earn this badge.
Expert Attested
A named human expert has provided an explicit attestation for the full stated result and proof version. The record links to the attestation and its scope. This does not imply journal peer review or institutional endorsement.

A check mark means qualifying evidence is recorded for the current proof version. “Not recorded” is an absence of evidence, not a claim that the result is false. Earlier-version, partial and withdrawn checks do not qualify as current verification. Originality remains a separate assessment. Labelled demonstration records are not published research results and do not claim completed reviews.

Search filters use all-of matching: selecting Lean Verified and Expert Attested shows only records with both. AI review is the publication baseline, not an optional filter.

Every status has a scope.

Badges must point to a specific proof version and supporting evidence. A change to the proof requires renewed checks for that version. Corrections and withdrawals remain part of the record; the preview does not yet accept public attestations.
